Northgate Voice Desk

A fictional client hires you to turn your Record → n8n → Play prototype into a voice receptionist they can run their clinic on. Work through seven missions the way a paid engagement runs: brief, build, client change requests, hardening, handover. Each mission ends with a test you can show someone.

The client brief

Northgate Physio, three therapists, one overloaded front desk

The problem

They take 60–90 calls a day. About a quarter are missed at lunch and after 17:00. Most missed calls are bookings, reschedules and "are you open Saturday?"

What they want

  • A voice assistant that answers clinic questions
  • Books, moves and cancels appointments
  • Sends reminders and a morning summary to staff
  • Hands anything urgent to a human, never guesses

Constraints

  • Store only name, phone and appointment time. No medical notes.
  • Symptoms like chest pain get a fixed safety script, not an AI answer.
  • No surprise bills: they approve any paid service first.

Your stack

n8n, ElevenLabs Speech to Text and Text to Speech (later ElevenLabs Agents), one LLM inside the AI Agent node, Google Sheets or Postgres for the diary, Telegram or Slack for staff alerts.

Turn it into proof you can sell

  • One-page case study: problem, architecture diagram, the four targets above with your measured numbers.
  • A short demo video: a booking by voice, a failure handled, the staff alert arriving.
  • Public repo with sanitised workflow JSON, .env.example and the README. No keys, no real phone numbers.
  • Three proposal openers for real job posts, each pointing at the part of this build that matches their problem.
House rules for every mission
  1. Write the acceptance test before you build. If you can't say how you'll prove it works, you haven't understood the task.
  2. Name nodes by what they do: STT · ElevenLabs, not HTTP Request1.
  3. Keys live in n8n credentials or environment variables, never pasted into a node's URL or body.
  4. Export the workflow JSON and commit it at the end of each mission, with a message saying what changed.
  5. When a client twist lands, write a one-line reply to the "client" first, then build.
